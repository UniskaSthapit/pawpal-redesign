Report · Prevent · Educate

Ending animal cruelty starts with someone speaking up.

Most cruelty isn't dramatic. It's a dog left in a yard with no shelter, a cat nobody has fed for a fortnight, too many animals in one house. If something looks wrong, report it — you don't need proof, and you don't need to be certain.

Emergency

An animal is in immediate danger

Locked in a hot car, being harmed, badly injured or unable to stand: call your state's RSPCA inspectorate or animal welfare authority now. If a person is at risk, call 000. Don't break into a vehicle or property yourself.

Non-urgent

Ongoing neglect or concern

Report to your state's animal welfare authority or local council. You can also send the details to PawPal below and our team will follow up with the right agency.

Stray or lost

Found a stray animal?

Take it to a vet or council pound to be scanned for a microchip, or contact your nearest PawPal shelter.

What to include in a report

  • The exact address or location, and how to find the animal
  • What kind of animal, and how many
  • What you've seen, and when — dates and times help
  • Photos or video, if you can take them safely
  • Whether it's ongoing or a one-off

What counts as cruelty

Cruelty isn't only deliberate violence. In every Australian state and territory, failing to provide an animal's basic needs — food, water, shelter and veterinary treatment — is an offence in its own right. Abandonment, keeping animals in unsuitable conditions and causing unnecessary pain are all covered. Laws differ between states, so check the legislation that applies where you live.

What happens after you report

People often hesitate because they don't know what they're setting in motion. Usually it's far less dramatic than they fear. An inspector assesses the report, may visit, and most cases end in advice, a direction to get veterinary treatment and a follow-up visit. Animals are only removed where there's no safe alternative.

Warning signs

  • Very thin, limping or with untreated wounds
  • Kept constantly chained or confined with no shelter
  • No access to clean water
  • Heavily matted coat or overgrown nails
  • Many animals kept in unhygienic conditions
