About PawPal

Every pet deserves a home that fits.

PawPal helps Australian shelters match animals with the right families. Adopters search and apply in minutes, shelter teams review applications with clear, explained information, and everyone knows what happens next.

How it started

PawPal began with a dog who came back twice

In 2024 a shelter volunteer watched the same young kelpie return twice in five months. Both families had loved him. Neither had understood that a working dog in a second-floor apartment, alone for ten hours a day, was never going to settle. Nothing on his listing had told them: it showed a photograph, a breed and an age.

That is a common story, and it is rarely anybody's fault. Shelters are stretched, listings get written in a hurry, and adopters make one of the biggest decisions of an animal's life from three lines of text. The information that would have prevented both returns already existed — the care team knew he needed hours of exercise and hated being alone. It simply never reached the people reading the ad.

PawPal was built to close that gap. Every observation the care team records becomes a structured field: energy level, whether a yard is needed, how they are with children and other animals. Those same fields drive the filters, the profile page and PawPal's matching — so what staff know is what adopters see.

The kelpie was adopted a third time, by a courier who drives with him all day. He has been home two years.

    Two returns of the same dog make it obvious that listings miss the information that matters most.

  2. 2025 — structured profiles

  3. 2026 — matching & tracking

    Explained matching, online applications and a full adoption timeline go live.

How we help

Six ways PawPal changes an animal's outcome

Rehoming is only part of it. Prevention, education and keeping animals with the owners they already have matter just as much.

Rescue & rehabilitation

Animals arrive as strays, surrenders or from cruelty cases. They are health-checked, treated and cared for before they are listed.

Honest matching

Explained compatibility and profiles that say what is unknown rather than guessing. Fewer mismatches means fewer returns.

Foster care

Kittens, puppies, nervous adults and recovering animals do far better in a home than a kennel.

Cruelty prevention

Reporting, community desexing and education. Most concerns end in advice and support rather than prosecution.

Keeping pets with owners

Pet food support, subsidised vet care and temporary fostering help people through hardship without surrendering the animal they love.

Care after adoption

A vet finder, first-year guidance and a team you can still call six months later.
